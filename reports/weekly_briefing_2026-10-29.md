## 4. Something I Should Know: Practical Civic Education (Track 3)
### Topic 2: How Local Governments Hire Software Developers
*Understand the jurisdictional classes (Competitive, Non-Competitive, Exempt, Unclassified), public authorities, and contractor vehicles used to staff public technology teams.*

#### 1. The Four Jurisdictional Classes of Civil Service
All civil service positions in New York State and local governments fall into one of four jurisdictional classes defined in Civil Service Law § 35-44: 1) Competitive Class (default; requires examination or NY HELPS waiver); 2) Non-Competitive Class (examinations not practicable; candidates must meet stated qualifications, e.g., specialized software architects or NYC IT Project Specialists); 3) Exempt Class (confidential or policy-making positions appointed at discretion); and 4) Labor Class (unskilled manual labor). In addition, higher education instructional staff and certain academic professionals fall into the Unclassified Service.

#### 2. Why Municipal Software Engineering Titles Vary
Unlike the private sector where 'Software Engineer' is ubiquitous, government titles reflect legacy civil service classification standards. New York State ITS uses 'Information Technology Specialist' (ITS 1 = SG-14, ITS 2 = SG-18, ITS 3 = SG-23, ITS 4 = SG-25) with parenthetics like (Programming), (Database), or (Systems). New York City uses 'Computer Associate (Software)', 'Computer Specialist (Software)', and modern non-competitive titles like 'IT Project Specialist' and 'IT Infrastructure Engineer'. Nassau and Suffolk counties use 'Programmer Analyst', 'Senior Programmer Analyst', and 'Network & Systems Specialist'.

#### 3. Public Authorities: The Direct-Hire Path
Public benefit corporations such as the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA/LIRR), Port Authority of NY & NJ (PANYNJ), New York Power Authority (NYPA), and Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL) operate outside traditional civil service commissions. They conduct direct recruitment using standard corporate job postings, applicant tracking systems (Workday, SuccessFactors), and technical coding interviews without civil service exam delays.

#### 4. IT Staff Augmentation: HBITS and PBITS Contractors
State and local agencies frequently augment their internal software engineering teams using centralized backdrop contracts managed by NYS OGS. Hourly Based Information Technology Services (HBITS) contracts allow agencies to quickly retain software developers, data engineers, and DevOps specialists through approved staffing vendors at standardized billable rates. Many technologists transition from HBITS vendor placements into permanent civil service or NY HELPS appointments.

#### Key Civil Service Terms to Know:
- **Non-Competitive Class:** Positions where competitive examination is found not practicable, but candidates must meet minimum training and experience qualifications.
- **Unclassified Service:** Civil service positions comprising elected officials, legislative officers, and academic/faculty/professional staff at SUNY and CUNY.
- **Public Benefit Corporation:** A corporate entity created by the state legislature to construct or operate public infrastructure (e.g., MTA, PANYNJ) outside direct state agency constraints.
- **HBITS / PBITS:** Hourly / Project Based Information Technology Services contracts established by NYS OGS for rapid IT consulting procurement.

#### Official Statutory References:
- [Classified Service Jurisdictional Classes](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVP/A3TBE) (`NYS Civil Service Law § 40-44`)
- [Metropolitan Transportation Authority Act](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PAA) (`NYS Public Authorities Law § 1260 et seq.`)
