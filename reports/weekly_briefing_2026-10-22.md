## 4. Something I Should Know: Practical Civic Education (Track 3)
### Topic 1: How Civil-Service Examinations and Eligible Lists Function
*Understand the constitutional merit system, the Rule of Three, continuous recruitment, eligible list lifespans, and why NY HELPS represents an unprecedented window of opportunity.*

#### 1. Constitutional Merit & The Rule of Three
Article V, Section 6 of the New York State Constitution mandates that appointments and promotions in the civil service of the state and all of its civil divisions shall be made according to merit and fitness, to be ascertained, as far as practicable, by examination. Under New York State Civil Service Law § 61(1), whenever an agency seeks to fill a competitive vacancy from an eligible list, the appointment must be made from among the top three (3) individuals certified as standing highest on the list who are willing to accept the position. This is known as the 'Rule of Three' or 'one-in-three rule'. It prevents nepotism by restricting an appointing officer's choice to the highest-scoring available candidates.

#### 2. How Scores and Banding Determine Your Reachability
Examinations yield a final score combining raw test results, veterans' credits (5 points for non-disabled, 10 for disabled veterans), and seniority points. Because dozens of candidates may score identical grades (e.g., a score of 100 or 95), Civil Service Law groups all individuals with the same score into the same rank. Under § 61, all candidates in the top three score bands are legally reachable. For instance, if List Rank 1 has 1 person, Rank 2 has 2 people, and Rank 3 has 15 people, all 18 people are technically reachable for appointment.

#### 3. The Life Cycle of an Eligible List: Canvass Letters to Expiration
Once an eligible list is established by the Civil Service Department or Commission, it typically remains active for four (4) years (NYS Civil Service Law § 56), though commissions may extend them up to additional years or terminate after one year if fewer than three candidates remain. When an agency has an opening, it issues 'Canvass Letters' (by mail or electronic portal) to reachable candidates asking: 'Are you available and interested in this position at this salary and location?' Candidates MUST respond by the stated deadline (usually 10-15 business days). Declining, failing to respond, or stating salary requirements higher than the job allows the agency to move past your name to the next reachable candidate.

#### 4. Open-Competitive vs. Continuous Recruitment Examinations
Traditional Open-Competitive exams are announced periodically (often once every 2 to 4 years), have a strict 3-to-4-week filing window, and test on a single Saturday or testing window. If you miss that filing window, you cannot take the exam until the next multi-year cycle. In contrast, Continuous Recruitment (CR) examinations accept applications on an ongoing basis with no deadline. Candidates are evaluated via standardized Training & Experience (T&E) questionnaires or periodic testing, and their names are continuously merged onto an active, rolling eligible list according to their score. Each candidate remains on the list for a fixed period (typically 1 to 2 years from their individual evaluation date).

#### 5. The NY HELPS Revolution: Bypassing the Traditional Exam
To combat severe post-pandemic public sector vacancies, the New York State Civil Service Commission authorized the New York Hiring for Emergency Limited Placement Statewide (NY HELPS) program. Running through June 30, 2028, NY HELPS temporarily waives competitive written examinations for thousands of entry- and mid-level titles (including Information Technology Specialist 1, 2, 3, and 4). Candidates apply directly to agencies based solely on meeting minimum education/experience qualifications. #### Key Civil Service Terms to Know:
- **Competitive Class:** Civil service positions for which it is practicable to determine the merit and fitness of applicants by competitive examination.
- **Rule of Three (Civil Service Law § 61):** Statutory rule requiring appointments from competitive lists to be made from among the top three ranking persons on the certified eligible list willing to accept the post.
- **Eligible List:** A roster of candidates who passed a civil service examination, ranked by final score plus statutory veteran/seniority credits.
- **Continuous Recruitment (CR):** An examination open for application continuously, allowing qualified candidates to be evaluated and merged onto an active list at any time.
- **Canvass Letter:** An official inquiry sent by a hiring agency to list eligibles to determine their willingness to accept an appointment at a specific agency, location, and salary.
- **Provisional Appointment:** A temporary appointment made to a competitive position when no viable eligible list exists. A provisional employee must pass the eventual competitive exam and score among the top three reachable candidates to keep the job.
- **NY HELPS:** New York Hiring for Emergency Limited Placement Statewide. Emergency waiver program waiving civil service exams for thousands of state and local titles through June 30, 2028.
