## 4. Something I Should Know: Practical Civic Education (Track 3)
### Topic 3: How Municipal Contracts and Tech Procurements Are Awarded
*Understand IFBs, RFPs, discretionary purchasing thresholds, M/WBE & SDVOB goals, and how a solo developer or boutique consultancy can win public contracts.*

#### 1. Sealed Bids (IFB) vs. Competitive Proposals (RFP)
Public procurement in New York is governed by State Finance Law § 163 (state agencies) and General Municipal Law § 103 (local governments). An Invitation for Bids (IFB) is used for standardized goods/commodities and must be awarded strictly to the lowest responsive and responsible bidder. A Request for Proposals (RFP) is used for complex services (like custom software development, cloud migration, or systems integration) where award is made based on 'Best Value'—evaluating technical approach, past performance, security architecture, and cost.

#### 2. Discretionary Purchasing: The Small Business Fast Track
Agencies are not required to conduct full competitive formal bids for small procurements. Under NYS State Finance Law § 163(6), state agencies may make discretionary purchases up to $50,000 (up to $85,000 for OGS centralized commodities) without formal competition. Even more powerful: state agencies may make discretionary purchases up to $750,000 to $1,500,000 when purchasing from certified Minority- and Women-Owned Business Enterprises (M/WBEs), Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Businesses (SDVOBs), or NYS Small Businesses. NYC has an equivalent $1.5M M/WBE non-competitive threshold in PASSPort.

#### 3. The New York State Contract Reporter (NYSCR)
By law (Economic Development Law Article 4-C), state agencies, authorities, and SUNY campuses must publish all procurement opportunities of $50,000 or greater in the New York State Contract Reporter (nyscr.ny.gov) at least 15 business days prior to the bid submission deadline. Registering an account is 100% free and allows vendors to receive daily automated alerts matching their NAICS/commodity codes.

#### Key Civil Service Terms to Know:
- **Best Value:** A procurement basis that optimizes quality, cost, and efficiency, rather than solely selecting the lowest dollar bid.
- **Discretionary Purchase:** Purchases below statutory dollar thresholds made without formal competitive sealed bidding, often reserved for small businesses and M/WBEs.
- **Vendor Responsibility (VendRep / PASSPort):** A mandatory legal review assessing a vendor's financial integrity, legal compliance, and past operational performance before a government contract can be approved.
